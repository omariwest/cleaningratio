Lysol Dilution Ratio Chart: Spray Bottle and Cleaning Mixes

“Lysol” is not one dilution. Ready-to-use trigger sprays, pourable multi-surface cleaners, and disinfectant concentrate use different directions. This guide separates them so you can scale the correct product instead of guessing from the brand name.

Quick answer

For current U.S. Lysol Clean & Fresh Multi-Surface Cleaner Pourable, Lysol lists 2 fl oz (1/4 cup) per gallon of warm water for general cleaning. That equals about 0.25 fl oz for 16 oz water, 0.375 fl oz for 24 oz, and 0.5 fl oz for 32 oz.

That ratio does not apply to every Lysol product. Lysol All-Purpose Cleaner trigger sprays are ready-to-use products, while Lysol Disinfectant Concentrate has its own concentrate label. If you are sanitizing or disinfecting rather than simply cleaning, follow the exact contact-time and dilution directions on your bottle.

First: which Lysol product do you have?

This is the step that prevents most bad Lysol ratios. The front of the bottle may say Lysol, but the back label decides whether the product is diluted at all.

Do not copy a ratio from a floor-cleaner article onto a trigger disinfectant spray. “Same brand” does not mean “same concentration.”

Lysol general-cleaning dilution chart: 16 oz to 1 gallon

The chart below scales the current U.S. Clean & Fresh Multi-Surface Cleaner direction of 2 fl oz cleaner per 1 gallon of water. The water amount is shown first because the label direction is written per gallon of water, not as an exact finished-bottle volume.

If your bottle is exactly 16, 24, or 32 oz and you need the finished mixture to stop at that line, add the measured cleaner first and use slightly less water. Do not overflow the bottle just to preserve a “per gallon of water” example literally.

Can you put diluted Lysol in a spray bottle?

Only when the label for your specific pourable product allows that application. Lysol's current surface guidance shows a stronger product-specific dilution for sanitizing or disinfecting and says it may be applied with a cloth, sponge, mop, or trigger spray. That is a different job from the lighter 2-oz-per-gallon general-cleaning mix.

The important distinction is: general cleaning, sanitizing, and disinfecting are not interchangeable directions. If you need a disinfecting claim, use the exact dilution and wet contact time printed on the product label. Do not dilute a ready-to-use Lysol trigger spray just to make it last longer; dilution can move it away from the labeled performance.

For ordinary non-disinfecting bottle math, the 32 oz Spray Bottle Dilution Guide shows how to translate a label rate without changing what the product label actually says.

How to scale a Lysol label without guessing

  1. Identify the exact product. Pourable concentrate, trigger spray, and disinfectant concentrate are different.
  2. Identify the job. Cleaning, sanitizing, and disinfecting can have different concentrations and contact times.
  3. Read the label unit. If it says ounces per gallon, scale that rate. If it gives a fixed ratio, use that ratio instead.
  4. Measure the cleaner. A small medicine cup or graduated measure is more reliable than estimating a capful.
  5. Add the correct water amount. Use warm water only where the label specifies it.
  6. Label a secondary bottle. Write the product name, dilution, and purpose so it is not confused with another cleaner.

If the back label is written in a format you do not recognize, use How to Read Cleaning Dilution Instructions on Labels before mixing.

Lysol dilution for mopping and floors

The 2-fl-oz-per-gallon general-cleaning direction is useful for larger washable, hard, non-porous surfaces when the surface and product label allow it. Lysol also publishes floor guidance for polished or non-porous wood using its Clean & Fresh Multi-Surface Cleaner, but that does not make the formula universal for every wood, stone, laminate, or coated floor.

Check the floor manufacturer first. Natural stone, unsealed wood, waxed finishes, and specialty coatings can require a different cleaner. If a floor dries tacky or streaky, do not automatically add more product; excess cleaner can create residue. Use the label amount, clean mop water, and a rinse pass when the product or surface instructions call for one.

Practical measuring for small bottles

Small spray bottles expose one problem with gallon directions: the concentrate amount can become tiny. For the 2-oz-per-gallon general-cleaning rate, a 16 oz water amount needs only 0.25 fl oz, which is about 1.5 teaspoons or 7.4 mL. A 32 oz water amount needs 0.5 fl oz, about 1 tablespoon or 14.8 mL.

Use a marked measuring cup, medicine cup, or syringe-style measure that is dedicated to cleaning products. Do not use kitchen measuring tools and then return them to food preparation. If the amount is too small to measure reliably, make a larger batch at the same label rate rather than estimating.

Do not mix Lysol with other cleaners

Keep the Lysol solution by itself. Do not combine it with bleach, vinegar, ammonia, alcohol, or another household cleaner unless the product label explicitly instructs you to do so. Mixing products can create hazardous fumes or unpredictable chemistry, and it also makes the labeled dilution meaningless.

Common questions

How much Lysol do I use in 1 gallon of water?

For current U.S. Lysol Clean & Fresh Multi-Surface Cleaner general cleaning, Lysol lists 2 fl oz (1/4 cup) per gallon of warm water. Other Lysol products can use different directions.

How much Lysol goes in a 32 oz spray bottle?

If you are scaling the same 2-fl-oz-per-gallon general-cleaning direction, 32 fl oz of water takes about 0.5 fl oz (14.8 mL) cleaner. Only use that in a spray bottle if your exact product label allows the application.

Can I dilute Lysol All-Purpose Cleaner trigger spray?

Treat the trigger product as ready-to-use unless its label says otherwise. The dilution chart on this page is for a pourable multi-surface cleaner direction, not a blanket rule for every Lysol spray.

Is the disinfecting ratio the same as the cleaning ratio?

No. Current Lysol surface guidance gives different directions for general cleaning versus sanitizing or disinfecting. If you need germ-kill performance, follow the exact product label, concentration, pre-cleaning requirement, and wet contact time.
